# EUframes_person_full.csv – codebook

A **fully synthetic** person-level companion to the EU-frames case: 41,660
simulated respondents spread across the real 270 country-year cells, with
every individual variable that the original author's published regressions
use. No Eurobarometer respondent appears in it, and no record in it is derived
from any one real person.

Generated by `companion/_model-outputs/simulate_person_joint.R`. The
[simulation module](../companion/simulation.qmd) explains the construction and
[the individual-level page](../companion/individual-level.qmd) fits the published specification to it.

## Validation against the real respondents {#what-makes-it-usable}

The evidence that this file is usable comes from a comparison made after it
was built. The same model was fitted to the real respondents
and to this file, and the two sets of coefficients were compared one by one. That
comparison is committed as
`companion/_model-outputs/twin_validation.csv`. In it, 60 of 64 coefficients
agree within two standard errors of the difference, and 46 within one.

The comparison uses the **two-sample** standard error of each difference, so a
difference counts as a disagreement only if it exceeds the sampling noise of this file. Judged instead against the
precision that 416,698 real respondents afford, 39 of the 64 agree. This file
holds a tenth as many people, so it can stand in for the real file only at its
own, lower precision.

## How it is built

The file is built in two stages.

**Who is in the sample.** A respondent's characteristics are one draw from a
latent multivariate normal distribution whose correlation matrix was estimated on the real
respondents. Each observed variable is a monotone transform of one coordinate of that draw:
sex and employment status by a threshold, urbanisation by cutpoints, age,
education and left-right through their quantile functions. Every marginal
distribution is reproduced by construction, and so is every pairwise
association. Age and being unemployed correlate at −0.24, age and education at
−0.14, and a generator that drew them independently would return the wrong
coefficients however carefully the effect of each variable was set.

Occupation beyond the unemployed indicator comes from a multinomial model
conditional on the rest.

**What they say.** Each of the thirteen framing items has a latent propensity
that is linear in the respondent's characteristics, shifted by their
country-year, and has a residual that is correlated with those of the other
twelve, which makes the model a multivariate probit. Because the generator
simulates answers to the thirteen items and derives the four dimension scores
from them, the scores have exact zeros and ones and sum to one automatically.

**The country-year shift is solved by iteration.** A dimension score is a
share of a respondent's own mentions, so raising the items in one dimension
lowers every other share. For each of the 270 cells the generator solves four
shifts by Newton iteration against a fixed reference sample, until the expected
dimension means differ from those in the panel by less than 1e-4.

## Sources of the parameters {#estimated-calibrated-authored}

**Estimated from the public panel** (`EUframes_cy.csv`, committed): the four
dimension means in every cell, which serve as the targets of the Newton iteration.

**Calibrated from the licensed person-level file**, read once and locally by
`calibrate_joint.R`, `calibrate_items.R` and `fit_person_anchor.R`: 171
characteristic correlations, the marginal distributions, 195 item probit
coefficients, 78 residual correlations, the conditional occupation model, and
the prevalence gradient. All are aggregate statistics of the kind that a
published appendix reports, and all are committed under
`companion/_model-outputs/`. The licensed extract itself is never committed.

**Authored** – three constants corresponding to nothing measured, none of them
an effect on what a respondent answers. Every individual-level effect in the
file is estimated. The three govern who enters the sample and how many: the
cell size as a fraction of the real respondent count (0.10), the fieldwork
selection tilt that gives `w1` something to correct (0.15 per decade below the
mean age), and the switch that makes that tilt vary with the unemployment rate
in a cell (0 by default).

## Variables

| Variable | Description |
|---|---|
| `pid` | Simulated person identifier |
| `cntry` | ISO 3166 two-letter code, as in `EUframes_cy.csv` |
| `year` | Year (2004–2013) |
| `w1` | Selection weight, the reciprocal of the fieldwork tilt, mean 1 within country-year. **Not a survey weight** – see the caution below |
| `age` | Simulated age in years, from the calibrated quantile grid |
| `female` | Simulated indicator at the calibrated marginal |
| `edu` | Age at which full-time education finished, the form on which the published band cut is made |
| `edu3` | Education band: `low` (< 16), `mid` (16–19), `high` (> 19) |
| `ses` | Occupational position, eight categories as published: `manual`, `self_employed`, `manager`, `white_collar`, `house_person`, `unemployed`, `retired`, `student` |
| `unemployed` | Indicator, equal to `ses == "unemployed"` |
| `urban` | `rural`, `small_town`, `large_town` |
| `rile` | Left–right self-placement, 1–10. **Missing for four of the sixteen waves**, mirroring the real data, so about 40% of rows are NA |
| `k` | Number of framing items that the respondent mentioned (0–13) |
| `cosmo`, `util`, `comm`, `lib` | Simulated dimension shares; sum to 1 for anyone who mentions anything, to 0 otherwise |
| `pos`, `neg` | Composites: `pos` = `cosmo` + `util`, `neg` = `comm` + `lib` |
| `unemp`, `growth`, `bailout` | Real published values, joined from `EUframes_cy.csv` |

## What it will not support

**Anything at the survey-wave level.** The real file has sixteen waves across
ten years; this one is built on the 270 country-years of the public panel and
its finest cluster is the country-year.

**The interacted form of the published model.** The validation uses the
specification without its cross-level interactions, the terms that let the
effects of unemployment and growth differ by education band. A synthetic file
built to reproduce a covariance structure reproduces such an interaction only if
one is built into it, and none was built into this one.

**Cell-level unemployment slopes are amplified by about a sixth.** The
generator gives each country-year four numbers, one shift per framing
dimension, where the real data has thirteen, one rate per item. A
dimension-level shift captures as little as 37% of the cell-to-cell variation
in some items, and what is lost shows up in two ways: the mentioning rate runs
about 1.6 points below the rate in the panel, and a model fitted to the *cell
means* of the twin returns unemployment slopes roughly 15% larger in magnitude
than those from the panel. The bias cannot be detected in a single draw,
because the sampling noise in one draw is the same size as the bias, and it
took a sweep of independent draws to separate it from zero. The individual-level validation above is unaffected,
because it compares person-level coefficients rather than cell-level ones.

Giving each cell its own thirteen item rates was tried and made things worse:
the marginals become exact and the coefficient comparison falls from 39
agreeing to 32, because the real item rates in a cell reflect its context and
its mix of people together, and the twin draws its people from pooled
marginals. Repairing it properly would mean reproducing the demographic
composition of each cell as well.

**Analyses of individual framing items.** The file reproduces the average
salience of each item but not its country-by-country variation, because
cell-level dimension means come from the public panel. Models that use the
four dimension scores are not affected.

**`w1` is not a survey weight.** It reverses an over-sampling of younger
respondents that the generator introduces so that weighting has something to
correct. It is a teaching device whose correct answer is known in advance. It
is not the Eurobarometer design weight and should not be read as one.

## Reproducing it

```
Rscript companion/_model-outputs/simulate_person_joint.R
```

from the project root. The generator reads only the public panel and the
committed calibration files, so anyone can run it. `$SIM_SEED` selects the
draw, `$SIM_OUT` the destination, and `$SIM_SWEEP` appends one row per outcome
to a file for repeated-draw studies.

aggregate statistics of the kind that the usage regulations describe as
"summarizing representations of the data typical to scientific works and
presentations", so releasing them is provided for. The regulations are then
silent on synthetic derivatives: no clause addresses whether a generated file
containing none of the original records falls inside or outside "the provided
data". Read on the ordinary meaning of that phrase, it falls outside, which is
why this file is released under CC BY. Releasing fully synthetic data derived
from restricted microdata is long-established practice – the SIPP Synthetic
Beta from the US Census Bureau works this way. Anyone redistributing this file
should include this whole paragraph with it, and not only its conclusion.
